GC/MGC DISCRETIONARY-TO-SYSTEMATIC STRATEGY SPECIFICATION — V0.2

Role framing: Senior Quant Developer + Senior Quant Researcher
Status: Research specification draft for review before implementation
Main objective: Convert the discretionary POI-based gold futures strategy into an agent-ready systematic research plan.

================================================================================
0. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

This strategy should NOT be treated as “every POI retest is a trade.”

The POI is the anchor / decision zone. The real systematic strategy is:

    POI candidate generation + quantitative confluence selection + controlled execution testing.

The strategy first detects valid POIs created during intraday swing-breaking displacement moves. A valid POI is stricter than a normal ICT FVG because it requires BOTH:

    1. A classic ICT wick-to-wick FVG.
    2. A close-to-next-open gap in the same direction.

Once both conditions are true, the tradable zone is the full high-to-low range of the middle candle in the 3-candle FVG sequence.

The POI is valid only for the trading day on which it is created. The next trading day starts fresh with no carryover POIs.

POIs can be created from 1:00am to 12:00pm New York time. Execution is only allowed during:
    - London window: 3:00am-6:00am New York time.
    - New York window: 7:00am-12:00pm New York time.

This means the New York session may use POIs created earlier, for example at 2:00am New York time, as long as the POI was created on the same trading day and has not expired/been invalidated.

The system currently uses:
    - GC for analysis/signal generation.
    - MGC for execution/risk sizing.
    - 1-minute OHLCV bars as the primary research timeframe.

================================================================================
1. STRATEGY IDENTITY
================================================================================

1.1 Core Strategy Type

The strategy is an intraday gold futures POI-retest system.

It can support:
    - continuation trades,
    - reversal trades,
    - pullback trades.

However, these should not be treated as one generic entry type. They should be classified separately during research.

Recommended research classification:

    A. Continuation POI Retest
       Price breaks a swing, creates a valid POI during displacement, retraces into the POI,
       then trades in the original displacement direction.

    B. Reversal POI Retest
       Price interacts with a valid POI as a reversal zone, possibly after extension,
       exhaustion, or failure to continue.

    C. Pullback POI Retest
       This is likely a subcategory of continuation unless separately defined.
       Price displaces, creates POI, retraces, then continues.

1.2 Strategy Thesis

Gold futures often create intraday displacement moves during active sessions. When price breaks a meaningful swing high or swing low, the displacement may leave behind an imbalance zone. If that imbalance contains both a classic wick-to-wick FVG and a close-to-next-open gap, the middle candle becomes a high-interest POI. A later retest of this POI creates a candidate trade. The trade only becomes stronger when additional confluence features support continuation or reversal.

1.3 Research Philosophy

The goal is not to force the discretionary system into a fixed hard-coded strategy immediately.

The goal is to:
    1. Detect all valid POIs.
    2. Detect all same-day POI retests.
    3. Build a candidate-trade dataset.
    4. Engineer market/context/confluence features around each candidate.
    5. Study which conditions separate good POI retests from weak ones.
    6. Convert the strongest evidence into backtestable execution rules.

POIs are valid only for the trading day on which they are created.

Rule:
    - At the start of each new trading day, clear all previous POIs.
    - Do not carry POIs from yesterday into today.

Open implementation detail:

4. MARKET STRUCTURE REQUIREMENT
================================================================================

4.1 Core Market Structure Rule

At any given moment, the strategy considers recent swing highs and swing lows.

If price breaks a recent swing high or swing low, that creates a displacement event.

The strategy then checks whether a valid POI formed inside that swing-breaking displacement move.

Base sequence:

    1. Identify current/recent swing high and swing low.
    2. Price breaks either the swing high or swing low.
    3. During that swing-breaking displacement, identify whether a valid POI exists.
    4. If a valid POI exists, mark it.
    5. Wait for same-day price retracement back to the POI.
    6. Treat the retest as a candidate trade.

4.2 Swing High / Swing Low Detection

This is not fully locked yet and must be treated as a research parameter.

Candidate definitions:

    3-bar swing high:
        H[i] > H[i-1] and H[i] > H[i+1]

    3-bar swing low:
        L[i] < L[i-1] and L[i] < L[i+1]

    5-bar swing high:
        H[i] is greater than highs of two candles before and two candles after.

    5-bar swing low:
        L[i] is lower than lows of two candles before and two candles after.

Recommended:
    Test 3-bar, 5-bar, and possibly 7-bar swing definitions.
    Record how many POIs and retests each method generates.

4.3 Swing Break Definition

Bullish swing break:
    Price trades above a prior valid swing high.

Bearish swing break:
    Price trades below a prior valid swing low.

Potential implementation options:
    - wick break: high > swing_high or low < swing_low.
    - close break: close > swing_high or close < swing_low.

Recommended first version:
    Use wick break for candidate generation.
    Later compare against close break.

4.4 Displacement Move Definition

The displacement move is the price leg that breaks the swing high or swing low.

For first implementation:
    - The displacement direction is bullish if price breaks a swing high.
    - The displacement direction is bearish if price breaks a swing low.
    - The valid POI must appear within the candles leading into or including the swing break.

Open issue:
    The exact start of the displacement leg is not fully formalized.

Recommended first approximation:
    For a bullish break, define the displacement leg as the candles from the most recent swing low before the break up to the candle that breaks the swing high.
    For a bearish break, define the displacement leg as the candles from the most recent swing high before the break down to the candle that breaks the swing low.

This should be tested and visually validated against chart examples.

5. FINAL POI DEFINITION
================================================================================

5.1 Important Clarification

The strategy POI is NOT:
    - only a standard ICT FVG,
    - only a close-to-open gap,
    - only the wick-to-wick imbalance zone.

The strategy POI requires BOTH:
    1. A classic ICT wick-to-wick FVG.
    2. A close-to-next-open gap in the same direction.

Once both conditions are true, the full range of the middle candle becomes the POI.

5.2 Candle Indexing

Let the candidate middle candle be candle i.

Then:
    Candle i-1 = candle before the candidate POI candle.
    Candle i   = candidate POI candle / middle candle.
    Candle i+1 = candle after the candidate POI candle.

For candle i:

    O[i] = open of candle i
    H[i] = high of candle i
    L[i] = low of candle i
    C[i] = close of candle i

For the next candle:

    O[i+1] = open of candle i+1
    H[i+1] = high of candle i+1
    L[i+1] = low of candle i+1

For the previous candle:

    H[i-1] = high of candle i-1
    L[i-1] = low of candle i-1

5.3 Bullish POI Formula

A bullish POI candle exists when BOTH conditions are true:

Condition 1 — Classic bullish ICT FVG:

    L[i+1] > H[i-1]

This means the low of the candle after the POI candle is above the high of the candle before the POI candle.

Condition 2 — Bullish close-to-next-open gap:

    O[i+1] > C[i]

This means the next candle opens above the close of the POI candle.

Therefore, candle i is a bullish POI candle if:

    L[i+1] > H[i-1]
    AND
    O[i+1] > C[i]

The bullish POI zone is:

    POI_high = H[i]
    POI_low  = L[i]
    POI_mid  = (H[i] + L[i]) / 2

5.4 Bearish POI Formula

A bearish POI candle exists when BOTH conditions are true:

Condition 1 — Classic bearish ICT FVG:

    H[i+1] < L[i-1]

This means the high of the candle after the POI candle is below the low of the candle before the POI candle.

Condition 2 — Bearish close-to-next-open gap:

    O[i+1] < C[i]

This means the next candle opens below the close of the POI candle.

Therefore, candle i is a bearish POI candle if:

    H[i+1] < L[i-1]
    AND
    O[i+1] < C[i]

The bearish POI zone is:

    POI_high = H[i]
    POI_low  = L[i]
    POI_mid  = (H[i] + L[i]) / 2

5.5 Gap Size and Tick Size

Gold futures tick size:
    tick_size = 0.10

Close-to-open gap in ticks:

    gap_ticks = (O[i+1] - C[i]) / tick_size

Bullish:
    gap_ticks > 0

Bearish:
    gap_ticks < 0

Recommended first implementation:
    Require at least 1 tick of close-to-next-open gap.

    Bullish:
        gap_ticks >= 1

    Bearish:
        gap_ticks <= -1

But this is a parameter and should be tested:
    min_gap_ticks = 1, 2, 3, etc.

5.6 FVG Size

Bullish FVG size:

    fvg_size = L[i+1] - H[i-1]

Bearish FVG size:

    fvg_size = L[i-1] - H[i+1]

In ticks:

    fvg_size_ticks = fvg_size / tick_size

Recommended:
    Record fvg_size_ticks for every POI.
    Do not over-filter at first.
    Later test whether larger/smaller FVGs perform differently.

5.7 POI Size

POI size:

    poi_size = H[i] - L[i]
    poi_size_ticks = poi_size / tick_size

This matters because POI size affects stop distance and entry quality.

6. POI VALIDITY AND EXPIRY
================================================================================

6.1 Validity Requirements

A POI is valid only if:

    1. It forms between 1:00am and 12:00pm NY.
    2. It is the middle candle of a valid 3-candle ICT FVG.
    3. It also has a close-to-next-open gap in the same direction.
    4. The FVG/gap direction matches the swing-break displacement direction.
    5. It is inside the swing-breaking displacement move.
    6. It forms on the current trading day.
    7. It has not expired, been invalidated, or already been used depending on the test rules.
    8. It is not created during known no-trade conditions such as major news or rollover danger if those exclusions are active.

6.2 Same-Day Expiry

All POIs expire at the end of the trading day.

Recommended initial expiry:
    - Expire all active POIs at 12:00pm NY after the trade window ends.
    - Alternatively, clear at NY midnight before the next POI search window.

Clarification needed:
    Should a POI created at 11:50am still be valid only until 12:00pm, or should it be ignored due to insufficient time left?

6.3 First-Touch vs Multi-Touch

Recommended first version:
    - Trade/analyze first retest only.
    - After the first touch, mark the POI as touched/used.
    - Do not generate repeated entries from the same POI in the first research version.

Later test:
    - Compare first touch vs second touch vs multiple touches.

================================================================================
7. POI RETEST / TOUCH DEFINITION
================================================================================

A retest occurs when a later candle’s price range intersects the POI zone.

For any later candle j after POI candle i:

    retest = H[j] >= POI_low AND L[j] <= POI_high

This means the candle overlaps the POI zone.

For a bullish POI retest:
    Price is usually retracing downward into the bullish POI.

For a bearish POI retest:
    Price is usually retracing upward into the bearish POI.

Entry variants should be tested separately:

    A. Boundary touch entry:
        Entry when price first touches the nearest POI boundary.

    B. 50% POI entry:
        Entry when price reaches POI_mid.

    C. Distal edge entry:
        Long: entry near POI_low.
        Short: entry near POI_high.

================================================================================
8. EXECUTION WINDOWS AND USE OF EARLIER POIS
================================================================================

The POI detection window and trade execution window are different.

POIs can form:
    1:00am-12:00pm NY.

Trades can execute:
    3:00am-6:00am NY.
    7:00am-12:00pm NY.

Therefore:
    - A POI created at 2:00am can be traded during London or NY if retested during the execution window.
    - A POI created during London may be traded later in NY.
    - A POI created before NY is still valid in NY if same-day and unexpired.

The agent must not restrict POI detection only to execution windows.

10. STOP-LOSS LOGIC
================================================================================

Current discretionary rule:
    - Stop goes above/below the POI.
    - Or above/below the candle before/after the POI.
    - Maximum stop = 100 ticks.
    - Minimum stop = 25 ticks.

10.1 Recommended First Stop Model

Use POI distal edge stop.

For long:
    stop = POI_low - stop_buffer

For short:
    stop = POI_high + stop_buffer

Recommended buffer:
    stop_buffer_ticks = 1 to 3 ticks, to be tested.

Stop distance:

    long_stop_ticks = (entry_price - stop_price) / tick_size
    short_stop_ticks = (stop_price - entry_price) / tick_size

Reject trade if:

    stop_ticks < 25
    or
    stop_ticks > 100

10.2 Stop Variants to Test Later

    A. POI distal edge stop.
    B. Adjacent candle stop.
    C. Swing structure stop.
    D. Fixed tick stop.
    E. ATR/range-adjusted stop.

12. CONFLUENCE / FEATURE ENGINEERING LAYER
================================================================================

This is where the strategy becomes quantitative.

The POI creates candidate trades. The confluence engine decides which candidates are higher quality.

Potential features:

12.1 VWAP Features

    - price_vs_vwap at POI creation
    - price_vs_vwap at retest
    - distance_from_vwap_ticks
    - distance_from_vwap_bps
    - vwap_slope
    - vwap reclaim/rejection
    - POI location relative to VWAP
    - retest occurring above/below VWAP

Example hypotheses:
    - Long continuation POIs perform better above VWAP.
    - Short continuation POIs perform better below VWAP.
    - Reversal POIs may perform better when price is extended far from VWAP.

12.2 Session Features

    - session label: pre-London, London, pre-NY, NY
    - time of day
    - minutes since London start
    - minutes since NY start
    - whether POI formed before session but retested during session
    - whether retest happens in high-volume window

12.3 Volume Features

    - current volume percentile
    - rolling volume percentile
    - volume at POI candle
    - volume during displacement move
    - volume at retest
    - retest volume relative to POI volume

12.4 Volatility Features

    - ATR / rolling range
    - realized volatility
    - bar range percentile
    - session range so far
    - distance moved from session open
    - volatility regime label

12.5 Market Structure Features

    - swing size
    - distance from broken swing
    - number of candles from swing break to POI
    - number of candles from POI creation to retest
    - whether market is making higher highs/lows or lower highs/lows
    - local trend direction
    - structural compression/expansion

12.6 POI Quality Features

    - fvg_size_ticks
    - close_open_gap_ticks
    - poi_size_ticks
    - candle body percentage
    - wick ratios
    - displacement strength
    - POI candle range percentile
    - whether POI candle is in direction of displacement
    - whether multiple POIs cluster near same zone

12.7 Extension / Exhaustion Features

    - distance from VWAP
    - distance from session open
    - distance from session high/low
    - move size before retest
    - number of consecutive candles in one direction
    - directional efficiency
    - whether price already made a large session move

12.8 Chop / Consolidation Features

    - directional efficiency
    - range compression
    - overlapping candle ratio
    - alternating candle ratio
    - wick-to-body ratio
    - rolling realized volatility vs rolling range

19.3 POI Expiry Boundary

User clarification:
    - POIs expire after 12:00pm New York time.
    - The next day starts fresh.
    - NY session can use levels marked earlier the same day, including around 2:00am NY.

Implementation decision:
    - POI detection window remains 1:00am-12:00pm NY.
    - Execution windows remain:
        London: 3:00am-6:00am NY.
        New York: 7:00am-12:00pm NY.
    - All active POIs expire at 12:00pm NY.
    - No POI carries past 12:00pm NY.
    - No POI carries into the next trading day.

Formal rule:
    A POI created at time t is eligible only if:
        1:00am <= t <= 12:00pm NY
        and
        same NY calendar date
        and
        current time <= 12:00pm NY
        and
        POI has not been invalidated by the chosen test rule.

Example:
    A POI created at 2:00am NY can be used during:
        - London window,
        - New York window,
    as long as the retest happens before 12:00pm NY.

Important implementation note:
    If a POI is created very close to 12:00pm NY, it still expires at 12:00pm NY.
    Later research can test whether to require minimum remaining time before expiry.

19.4 First Touch vs Multiple Touches

User clarification:
    - Multiple touches are allowed.
    - One touch is okay, but if price retests the POI again, that is also acceptable.

Implementation decision:
    - Do not permanently disable a POI after first touch by default.
    - Track touch count for each POI.
    - Allow the same POI to generate multiple retest events before expiry.
    - Each retest should be recorded separately in the retest table.

Required fields:
    - poi_id
    - retest_number
    - first_touch_flag
    - touch_timestamp
    - touch_session
    - time_since_poi_creation
    - time_since_previous_touch
    - whether price fully crossed the POI
    - whether prior touch already hit a theoretical target/stop under a given test variant

Research output:
    Compare:
        - first touch only,
        - second touch only,
        - third-or-later touch,
        - all touches,
        - first touch vs later touches by session and confluence.

Important:
    Multiple touches must be handled carefully to avoid overlapping trade assumptions.
    If a trade from the first touch is still open, the system should not open a second overlapping trade on the same POI unless a specific pyramiding/re-entry rule is later defined.

Recommended first rule:
    - Record all touches.
    - For trade simulation, avoid overlapping trades from the same POI unless explicitly testing re-entry logic.

19.5 Reversal Logic

19.6 Stop-Loss Model

User clarification:
    - Stop placement is tricky.
    - Both POI-based stops and adjacent-candle stops should be tested.

Implementation decision:
    - Implement multiple stop models as research variants.

Stop Model A — POI Distal Edge Stop:
    Long:
        stop = POI_low - buffer

    Short:
        stop = POI_high + buffer

Stop Model B — Adjacent Candle Stop:
    This requires careful indexing around the POI candle.

    Candidate variants:
        B1. Previous-candle reference:
            For long: stop below L[i-1]
            For short: stop above H[i-1]

        B2. Next-candle reference:
            For long: stop below L[i+1]
            For short: stop above H[i+1]

        B3. Most conservative adjacent reference:
            For long: stop below min(L[i-1], L[i], L[i+1])
            For short: stop above max(H[i-1], H[i], H[i+1])

        B4. Tightest adjacent reference:
            For long: stop below the nearest structurally logical adjacent low.
            For short: stop above the nearest structurally logical adjacent high.

Recommended first implementation:
    - Test POI distal edge stop.
    - Test conservative adjacent-candle stop.
    - Keep tighter discretionary adjacent variants for later after visual validation.

Stop constraints:
    - Minimum stop distance: 25 ticks.
    - Maximum stop distance: 100 ticks.
    - Reject trades outside this range.

Important:
    Stop model must be part of the experimental grid.
    Do not choose one final stop model before testing.

19.7 Risk Per Trade

        risk_pct = 0.005
        risk_dollars = 250

    Maximum risk:
        max_risk_pct = 0.01
        max_risk_dollars = 500

For MGC:
    Approximate tick value:
        $1 per tick per MGC contract

Position sizing:
    contracts = floor(risk_dollars / (stop_ticks * tick_value_per_contract))

For 0.5% risk:
    contracts = floor(250 / stop_ticks)

For 1.0% max risk:
    contracts = floor(500 / stop_ticks)

Risk constraints:

    - If calculated contracts < 1, reject the trade.
    - If risk exceeds 1.0%, reject or cap position size.

Research output:
    Record performance under:
        - fixed 0.5% risk,
        - capped maximum 1.0% risk,
        - 1 contract baseline,
        - possible fixed-contract sensitivity test.

Important:
    Since GC is the analysis instrument and MGC is the intended execution instrument, final realistic sizing should be based on MGC tick value.
