# 1. Executive Summary

Quant Project 1 is an independent quantitative research and trading-system development programme focused on CME Gold Futures (GC) and Micro Gold Futures (MGC). Its purpose is to convert market hypotheses and discretionary trading knowledge into explicit, causal, testable, and eventually executable rules; discover additional statistical structure from market data; and integrate the strongest validated components into a hybrid trading system.
The project is research-first. It does not begin from the assumption that an edge exists. The central objective is to determine whether any proposed behaviour remains economically meaningful after realistic execution costs, chronological out-of-sample testing, parameter sensitivity, risk constraints, and the operating rules of the selected futures funding evaluator or prop firm.
GC is the primary Phase 1 research instrument because it is the benchmark Gold futures contract and is the established analysis instrument in the repository. MGC is the intended initial execution instrument because it is one-tenth the size of GC, with materially smaller tick value and capital commitment. This makes MGC more suitable for controlled risk, prop-firm drawdown limits, and early-stage capital. GC execution may become appropriate later as capital, risk tolerance, and operational maturity increase.
The current repository already contains a validated data foundation, a liquidity-aware active-contract series, a formalized discretionary Point of Interest (POI) research branch, and an independent statistical-feature branch. However, it remains a research environment: no strategy has been approved for a sequential backtest or live trading, and production execution, position sizing, transaction-cost modelling, and GC-to-MGC execution mapping remain incomplete.

[TABLE START]
Product statement / Build a reproducible, risk-aware quantitative platform that translates market expertise into testable rules, independently searches for statistical structure, validates all candidate systems under realistic historical and forward conditions, and deploys only strategies that satisfy predeclared research, risk, prop-firm, and operational gates.
[TABLE END]


# 2. Vision, Mission, and Product Principles


## Vision

Develop a durable hybrid Gold-futures trading capability in which systematic research, disciplined execution, and informed market interpretation reinforce one another rather than compete.

## Mission

Independently research, engineer, validate, and operate one or more systematic intraday futures strategies that can be executed consistently by software, initially under the constraints of futures prop firms and ultimately with proprietary capital.

## Core principles

Evidence before conviction: market intuition may generate hypotheses, but only reproducible evidence can approve a rule for backtesting or deployment.
Causality before performance: every feature and signal must use only information available at the decision time.
Economic value before statistical novelty: a small p-value is not an edge unless the effect survives costs, drawdowns, and executable trade sequencing.
Simple before complex: interpretable, stable rules are preferred over fragile complexity with marginal in-sample gains.
Risk is a product requirement: position limits, daily loss controls, prop-firm constraints, and kill switches are part of the system design, not an afterthought.
Research and execution are separate layers: event studies, forward labels, and attractive charts are not equivalent to a stateful sequential backtest or live system.
No performance guarantee: the project may reject many ideas and may conclude that a proposed strategy has no durable edge.

# 3. Problem Statement and Opportunity


## The problem

Discretionary trading can contain genuine market insight, but it often relies on tacit judgement, inconsistent definitions, selective memory, and execution that varies with fatigue, emotion, or context. Purely statistical research can improve consistency, but it can also overfit historical noise, ignore market mechanics, or produce signals that are difficult to execute.
The project addresses this by building a common research and execution framework in which discretionary concepts are formalized into machine-readable rules, independent statistical features are evaluated without contamination from the discretionary branch, and all candidate systems are subjected to identical execution, risk, and validation standards.

## The opportunity

Gold futures offer a liquid, centrally cleared market with a benchmark contract (GC) and a smaller execution contract (MGC). The project can use the liquidity and price-discovery characteristics of GC for research while using the smaller MGC contract to express the same market thesis with finer risk control. The approach also creates a practical path from research to prop-firm capital, provided the system is explicitly engineered around each firm’s rules and does not rely on unrealistic fills or excessive leverage.

# 4. Objectives, Success Definition, and Non-Goals


## Primary objectives

1. Formalize discretionary Gold-futures concepts, including POIs, trend context, displacement, retests, invalidation, entries, stops, targets, and time exits, into deterministic definitions.
2. Independently research causal statistical features derived from one-minute OHLCV and time/session context without using POI-derived information during discovery.
3. Develop one or more stateful sequential backtests that model order timing, costs, slippage, ambiguity, position state, rollover, daily limits, and forced exits.
4. Validate whether signals discovered on GC can be mapped to MGC execution without unacceptable divergence, liquidity degradation, spread cost, or fill risk.
5. Create a configurable prop-firm rules engine that can simulate evaluation and funded-account constraints without hard-coding one provider.
6. Forward test the frozen strategy and risk engine in the Rithmic paper environment before any live or funded deployment.
7. Build a hybrid system only after the independent statistical branch and the discretionary POI branch have each been evaluated and frozen under their own rules.
8. Establish a maintainable collaborative codebase with versioned research artifacts, tests, documented decisions, and reproducible runs.

## Success is not defined as “finding a profitable backtest.”

A successful project produces a trustworthy research and execution process. A deployable strategy must show positive net expectancy and acceptable risk under predeclared rules, but rejecting an attractive-looking strategy because it fails validation is also a successful research outcome.

## Current non-goals

High-frequency trading, market making, latency arbitrage, or sub-millisecond competition.
Initial live execution in standard GC before capital and risk capacity justify the tenfold contract exposure.
Forex or CFD execution; the initial focus is exchange-traded futures with a consistent underlying market.
Overnight positions in the first intraday system.
Options, multi-asset portfolio construction, or cross-market statistical arbitrage in the initial scope.
MBO-based production research in Phase 1; order-book work is a future programme after storage, compute, data, and engineering budgets are approved.
Allowing discretionary overrides to alter live decisions without being logged, governed, and separately evaluated.

# 7. Market, Instrument, Data, and Timeframe Scope


## 7.1 Instrument policy


[TABLE START]
Instrument | Role | Rationale and constraints
GC — COMEX Gold Futures | Primary analysis and signal-research instrument | Benchmark contract, deeper liquidity, and established project research history. Each contract represents 100 troy ounces and the minimum $0.10/oz tick equals $10 per contract. This larger exposure increases margin, drawdown, and risk concentration.
MGC — Micro Gold Futures | Intended initial execution instrument | One-tenth the size of GC: 10 troy ounces and $1 per minimum tick. It provides finer position sizing, lower capital commitment, and better compatibility with early-stage and prop-firm risk limits.
GC live execution | Future scaling option | Permitted only after sufficient capital, stable live operations, and explicit risk approval. The system must not assume that a GC signal automatically transfers to MGC or vice versa.
[TABLE END]

Actual exchange and broker margin requirements change over time. The project will therefore store margin and buying-power assumptions as versioned configuration rather than as permanent constants. The structural reason for preferring MGC is the tenfold smaller contract exposure and tick value, not a single temporary margin figure.

## 7.2 Data assets

Source: Databento GLBX.MDP3 one-minute OHLCV for GC and MGC.
Coverage: 24 May 2021 to 24 May 2026; the current eligible statistical frame ends on 22 May 2026.
Research table: liquidity-selected, raw non-back-adjusted tradable prices with rollover and tradability controls.
Timestamp policy: UTC is the immutable join key; America/New_York is the business and session timezone.
Raw DBN is immutable. Parquet is the primary research format. Raw and processed market data are not committed to Git.

## 7.3 Timeframe policy

One-minute bars are the base research and entry-resolution dataset because the discretionary strategy historically looks for entries on the one-minute chart. Higher-timeframe context—such as 5-minute, 15-minute, 1-hour, and daily trend or volatility state—must be derived from completed lower-timeframe bars or independently sourced and time-aligned. A one-hour feature may use only a fully completed one-hour candle at the decision timestamp; partial future candle information is prohibited.

[TABLE START]
Critical limitation of one-minute OHLCV / A one-minute bar does not reveal the order in which its high and low occurred, queue priority, bid/ask spread, aggressor flow, or market depth. Any stop/target collision inside the same bar is ambiguous and must be handled conservatively, excluded, or later resolved with finer data.

# 9. Hybrid System Definition

“Hybrid” must be defined precisely. It must not become an untestable arrangement in which a human changes signals after seeing the outcome. The project will evaluate hybrid designs as separate, logged products with explicit decision authority.

[TABLE START]
Hybrid mode | Description | Research requirement
A. Systematic POI conversion | The algorithm fully encodes the discretionary setup and executes it without human confirmation. | Every formation, context filter, entry, stop, target, and invalidation rule must be deterministic.
B. Statistical filter/ranker | The POI system generates opportunities; independent statistical features filter, rank, size, or select direction. | The statistical feature set must be frozen before POI integration. Incremental out-of-sample value must be measured beyond the POI baseline.
C. Human-in-the-loop approval | The algorithm generates a structured signal and the trader approves or rejects it before order submission. | Approval criteria and reasons must be logged. Human-approved performance must be compared against fully systematic performance and against all generated opportunities.
D. Systematic core with discretionary risk veto | The strategy is systematic, but a narrowly defined risk veto may disable trading under exceptional conditions. | Veto conditions must be predeclared where possible; all interventions must be timestamped and independently reviewed.
[TABLE END]

The long-term target is likely Mode B or a controlled combination of Modes B and C: discretionary market structure formalized into candidate events, independent statistics used to estimate opportunity quality, and a clear boundary between automated decisions and human authority.

# 11. Quantitative Research Requirements


## 11.1 Observation and execution convention

Decision features end at completed bar t.
Theoretical entry is at the open of bar t+1 unless a different executable convention is explicitly defined.
Entry eligibility is determined by the entry timestamp, not merely the feature timestamp.
Fixed-horizon future paths begin at t+1 and are unavailable—not shortened—when the full path crosses an invalid boundary.
The initial fixed horizons are 5, 15, 30, 60, 120, and 180 minutes.

## 11.2 Chronological governance

Development: start of eligible data through 31 December 2023.
Validation: 1 January 2024 through 31 December 2024.
Final test: 1 January 2025 through 22 May 2026.
The final-test period has already had limited descriptive exposure and must not be described as fully unseen.
New definitions, thresholds, and models may not be tuned to exposed final-test outcomes. Forward paper data and newly collected live-market data provide the cleanest future holdout.

## 11.3 Statistical standards

Chronological splits only; random splitting of minute observations is prohibited.
Purging or embargo must be used where overlapping labels create train/test dependence.
Thresholds, bins, normalizers, and time-of-day baselines are fitted on Development only and reused unchanged out of sample.
Broad feature screens require false-discovery control, effect sizes, date coverage, and stability—not p-values alone.
Overlapping observations are correlated events, not independent trades; date-block bootstrap or equivalent dependence-aware methods are preferred.
Feature redundancy must be reduced before multivariate modelling. Interpretable representatives are preferred over duplicate transformations.
Simple linear/logistic benchmarks precede more complex tree or nonlinear models.
No strategy rule is approved from event-level average returns alone.

## 11.4 Candidate approval evidence

Before a signal becomes a backtest candidate, the research report must include its economic hypothesis, exact availability timing, sample size, date coverage, Development and Validation behaviour, session/year/regime breakdowns, effect size, uncertainty, friction-aware magnitude, parameter sensitivity, failure modes, and reasons for rejection or advancement.

# 12. Backtesting Requirements

A backtest must behave like a chronological trading process. It may not assume that every overlapping event can be traded independently or that maximum favourable excursion is realized profit.

[TABLE START]
Area | Required treatment
Order timing | Use next-bar or another explicitly executable convention. Prohibit same-bar fills that rely on a close known only after the order would have been submitted.
Intrabar ambiguity | When stop and target are both touched inside a one-minute bar, use conservative ordering, exclude the observation, or report a bounded scenario range. Optimistic ordering may be shown only as an upper bound.
Costs | Include commissions, exchange/clearing fees, spread, slippage, and a pessimistic stress case. Cost assumptions must be versioned by instrument and date.
Position state | Define one-position versus multi-position behaviour, pyramiding, re-entry, signal conflicts, side switching, and account-level exposure.
Session rules | Initial entries are restricted to London [03:00, 06:00) and New York [07:00, 12:00) America/New_York. No entries from 06:00–07:00 or at/after noon.
End of day | All positions must be flat no later than 15:30 New York time. No overnight holding in the initial product.
Rollover and tradability | No trade path may cross an invalid rollover, contract, continuous-segment, missing-bar, or non-tradable boundary.
Sizing | Evaluate fixed contract, volatility-targeted, fixed fractional, and prop-limit-aware sizing only after an explicit stop/risk unit exists.
Account model | Track realized/unrealized P&L, commissions, buying power, drawdown type, daily reset, and prop-rule state.
Validation | Report walk-forward or locked-period results, parameter sensitivity, cost sensitivity, Monte Carlo/path resampling, and failure-mode analysis.
[TABLE END]


## Minimum backtest outputs

Number of trades and active trading dates; long/short and session mix.
Net and gross expectancy per trade in R, ticks, and dollars.
Win rate, average/median win, average/median loss, profit factor, payoff ratio, and tail quantiles.
Equity curve, maximum drawdown, drawdown duration, daily P&L distribution, and time under water.
Performance by year, month, session, direction, volatility regime, and strategy version.
Turnover, exposure, average holding time, missed/invalid signals, and ambiguous-bar frequency.
Parameter and cost sensitivity, bootstrap confidence intervals, and Monte Carlo risk-of-breach estimates.
Prop-firm evaluation pass probability, expected time to pass, probability of daily/max-loss breach, and funded-period survival under the selected rule set.

# 13. Prop-Firm Constraint Engine

Prop firms are the intended initial pathway to operational capital and payouts, but their rules vary and can change. The system must therefore treat the prop firm as a versioned policy layer, not as a fixed assumption embedded in strategy code.

[TABLE START]
Rule family | Examples of required parameters

Loss constraints | Daily loss limit, maximum loss, static versus trailing drawdown, intraday versus end-of-day calculation, unrealized P&L treatment.

The selected firm’s current terms must be reverified before implementation and before each deployment. The project will not attempt to bypass, conceal, or exploit prohibited behaviour. Strategy design must comply with the firm’s published automation and trading rules.

# 14. Forward Testing and Rithmic Paper Trading

Rithmic is the preferred paper-trading environment because its simulator connects to live exchange market data and uses a simulated matching engine with server-side risk controls. This provides a materially stronger forward-test environment than replaying synthetic prices or using delayed data.
Paper trading still cannot prove live equivalence. It may not perfectly reproduce queue priority, market impact, all rejection paths, real brokerage latency, human operational errors, or the exact fills received in a funded account. Paper results must therefore be treated as a deployment gate, not as a guarantee.

[TABLE START]
Forward-test stage | Purpose | Exit criteria
Shadow mode | Run signals and risk checks against live data without transmitting orders. | No stale-data violations, deterministic decisions, complete telemetry, and reconciled theoretical fills.
Rithmic simulation | Submit simulated orders and validate order lifecycle, fill handling, slippage, position state, risk rules, and prop-policy state. | Frozen strategy, zero unexplained position mismatches, zero hard-rule breaches, stable operations, and adequate sample under predeclared criteria.
Prop evaluation — minimum size | Operate the exact approved configuration under the chosen evaluator’s terms. | Pass or fail according to the rules without changing the system mid-evaluation. Any material code/rule change restarts validation.
Funded account — controlled scale | Validate real operational and payout behaviour at the minimum contract size. | Sustained rule compliance, expected versus realized execution within tolerance, and no unresolved safety events.
Scale-up | Increase account count, MGC size, or later GC exposure. | Separate risk approval based on live evidence, capital, liquidity, and infrastructure capacity.
[TABLE END]

Provisional paper-test sample gate / Before the first paper campaign, the team will freeze a minimum sample based on strategy frequency. The default proposal is at least 60 trading days and at least 100 executed trades, with longer testing for low-frequency systems. The threshold must be declared before results are observed.

# 15. Live Execution, Risk Controls, and Observability


## 15.1 Execution requirements

The live system must separate signal generation, order management, risk management, and broker/gateway communication.
Every order must have a unique strategy, signal, account, and parent-decision identifier.
Acknowledgements, rejections, partial fills, cancels, replaces, and fills must be idempotently processed.
Positions must be reconciled against the broker/gateway state; unresolved mismatch forces liquidate-only or shutdown mode.
Strategy restarts must recover open orders, positions, daily P&L, and risk state from durable storage.
Manual intervention is permitted only through authenticated, logged controls.

## 15.2 Mandatory kill switches


[TABLE START]
Trigger | Required response
Stale or missing market data | Block new entries; cancel unsafe working orders; escalate if an open position cannot be managed.
Gateway disconnect or degraded order acknowledgements | Enter safe mode, prevent duplicate orders, reconcile on reconnect.
Position mismatch | Stop strategy, cancel working orders, reconcile, and require human review.
Daily internal loss limit reached | Flatten according to policy and disable trading for the remainder of the session/day.
Prop-firm buffer breached | Stop before the external hard limit; do not rely on the firm’s liquidation system as the primary control.
Abnormal slippage or fill quality | Reduce/disable trading and flag the execution regime for review.
Software exception or corrupted state | Fail closed: no new orders, preserve logs, and require controlled recovery.
Manual emergency stop | Immediate cancel/flatten action according to configured safety policy.
[TABLE END]

# 17. Collaboration and Engineering Workflow

1. Pull the latest main branch before beginning work.
2. Create a short-lived branch for one coherent research or engineering milestone.
3. State the research question, definition, expected artifacts, and acceptance checks before implementation.
4. Keep changes scoped; do not stage unrelated files or use broad add commands in a mixed working tree.
5. Run the complete automated test suite and any milestone-specific full-run validation.
6. Review schemas, row counts, missingness, date coverage, outputs, memory use, and research conclusions.
7. Update the relevant context report and README when the project state changes.
8. Open a pull request that explains the hypothesis, implementation, outputs, checks, limitations, and decisions.
9. Require review by at least one partner who did not author the change before merging.
10. Do not commit raw/processed market data, secrets, virtual environments, caches, generated bulk outputs, or local logs.

## Definition of done for a milestone

The intended research or engineering contract is implemented.
Tests pass and the relevant notebook/script executes without unexplained errors.
Outputs are saved in the agreed locations and are reload-validated where applicable.
Performance and memory behaviour are acceptable for the target data size.
No lookahead, data-boundary, or strategy-rule violations are found.
Documentation and project status are updated.
A reviewer can reproduce the result from the committed code and authorized data.

Phase 3 — Hybrid integration research | Compare statistical-only, POI-only, POI filtered/ranked by statistics, and any human-in-loop design. | Frozen hybrid specification demonstrating incremental out-of-sample value—or rejection.
Phase 4 — MGC execution and prop-policy layer | GC-to-MGC transfer validation, cost model, account/risk model, and selected prop-firm rule configuration. | A rule-compliant strategy configuration with realistic pass/fail simulation.
Phase 5 — Rithmic forward test | Shadow and simulated live-data operation, telemetry, fills, reliability, and risk controls. | Paper gate passed under predeclared duration/sample and operational criteria.
Phase 6 — Controlled prop deployment | Evaluation and funded operation at minimum size, followed by measured scale-up. | Rule compliance, execution reconciliation, and approved risk-adjusted live evidence.
Phase 7 — Granular market-data programme | Evaluate TBBO/MBP as intermediate steps, then MBO order-book reconstruction and order-flow research. | Separate PRD/data budget, validated book pipeline, and new research branch.
Phase 8 — Proprietary capital and GC scaling | Operate without prop restrictions when capital permits; consider GC or portfolio expansion. | Independent capital, institutionalized controls, and a new scaling risk review.
[TABLE END]


# 19. MBO, TBBO, and Order-Flow Future Scope

The future order-flow programme will be treated as a separate product expansion rather than an informal extension of the one-minute notebook. Databento MBO contains every order-book event across all price levels and is the highest-granularity public order-level schema available in this feed. TBBO is an L1 trade-space schema that combines each trade with the best bid and offer immediately preceding it. These datasets can support questions that OHLCV cannot answer, but they require materially greater storage, stateful book reconstruction, compute, and execution modelling.

[TABLE START]
Stage | Data | Potential research value | Why staged
1 | Trades / TBBO | Trade direction, spread at trade time, top-of-book liquidity, short-term impact, execution-cost estimation. | Lower storage and complexity than full order book; useful bridge from OHLCV.
2 | MBP-1 / MBP-10 | BBO/depth imbalance, microprice, liquidity depletion, depth changes, trade-to-book interaction. | Introduces stateful quote/depth logic without individual order queues.
3 | MBO | Individual order events, queue position, full-depth dynamics, cancellations/modifications, order-flow and execution simulation. | Highest granularity and greatest cost/engineering burden; justified only after a concrete hypothesis and budget exist.
[TABLE END]

MBO is not automatically predictive merely because it is more granular. The order-flow programme must still use causal features, chronological validation, realistic queue/fill modelling, and strict control of data-mining risk.

# 21. Risks and Mitigations


[TABLE START]
Risk | Impact | Mitigation
Overfitting and multiple testing | False edge and strategy failure out of sample. | Chronological governance, feature registry, false-discovery control, small frozen shortlist, sensitivity tests, and untouched forward data.
Lookahead leakage | Invalid performance and false confidence. | Availability timestamps, next-bar convention, boundary tests, code review, and synthetic leakage tests.
One-minute intrabar ambiguity | Incorrect stop/target ordering and overstated fills. | Conservative/excluded ambiguity policies, scenario bounds, and later granular data.
GC-to-MGC mismatch | Signal quality or fills degrade at execution. | Dedicated transfer study using synchronized prices, spread, liquidity, and slippage.
Prop-firm rule changes | Unexpected breach or unusable strategy. | Versioned policy configuration, effective dates, pre-deployment verification, and stricter internal buffers.
Simulator-to-live gap | Paper results fail in real operation. | Pessimistic assumptions, shadow testing, minimum live size, detailed telemetry, and gradual scale-up.
Operational failure | Duplicate orders, unhedged position, or account breach. | Idempotent order handling, reconciliation, durable state, kill switches, and fail-closed design.
Team knowledge imbalance | Market logic or research methodology is misunderstood. | Written specifications, paired reviews, partner onboarding, glossary, and decision records.
Data/licensing exposure | Unauthorized redistribution or loss of access. | Private repository, no data in Git, approved credentials, and explicit data access arrangements.
Researcher discretion after results | Hidden overfitting and irreproducibility. | Predeclared hypotheses/thresholds, immutable run artifacts, and logged decision rationale.
Capital pressure | Risk limits are loosened to pass evaluations quickly. | Risk budget is frozen before the evaluation; no mid-campaign strategy changes or revenge scaling.
[TABLE END]
